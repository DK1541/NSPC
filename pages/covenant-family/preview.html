Covenant Family

Not a membership. A promise we keep to each other.

We don't call ourselves a congregation of members. We call ourselves a Covenant Family — because what holds this place together is what we've promised one another, not what we've signed.

Mission & Values

Wherever you are on your faith journey, you are welcome here

We welcome you with open hearts and minds. This congregation holds a wide range of politics and theology, united by a commitment to follow Jesus — and by a conviction that the door stays open.

Creating a Spiritual Home, Growing the Covenant Family.
Our calling

Worship

We gather to worship God together — in the sanctuary through the school year, and outdoors at the garden all summer.

Welcome

We meet others with Christian compassion, whoever they are and whatever brought them to the door.

Educate

We promote faith development at every age, from Godly Play at two years old to Bible study on Wednesday mornings.

Advocate

We work for peace and justice, and we address suffering where we find it — starting with our own neighborhood.

Neighboring Initiative

Three times a year, the church spills onto the street

Not outreach aimed at the neighborhood. Events held with it — free, open, and no one asked what they believe at the door.

Easter Egg Hunt

The Saturday before Easter. Crafts, stories, and an egg hunt for every child who turns up.

Block Party

The Saturday before school starts. We close the street for food trucks, ice cream, games, and a bounce house.

Spring Clean Out

A free community exchange with Shorewood Buy Nothing, in the freecycle spirit — bring what you don't need, take what you do.

History

A hundred years on the same corner

From a storefront mission with Sunday School in the old village jail, to a centennial celebrated in 2022.

  1. 1922

    Begins as a mission of Westminster Presbyterian, meeting in a Shorewood storefront. The old village jail next to the village hall serves as the Sunday School room.

  2. 1923

    The congregation formally organizes.

  3. 1924

    The first building goes up — a wooden structure on the corner.

  4. 1951

    The name changes to North Shore Presbyterian Church.

  5. 1952

    The colonial-style brick building is dedicated — the sanctuary the congregation still worships in.

  6. 1959

    The educational wing is added.

  7. 2002

    The memorial patio is built — now home to summer worship at the garden.

  8. 2015

    The pineapple becomes our mark. It is the universal image of hospitality, which is the promise we most wanted to make in public.

  9. 2022

    One hundred years.
