History

A hundred years on the same corner

It began in a storefront, with Sunday School held in the old village jail. Everything since has happened within a few hundred feet of where it started.

1922 to now

One congregation, three buildings, one corner

Read down the line. The years in gold are the decisions; the small notes between them are how long the congregation lived with each one before making the next.

Finding a room 1922 – 1949
1922

A mission, in a shop

Westminster Presbyterian in Milwaukee starts a mission in Shorewood. Services are held in a storefront. Sunday School meets in the old village jail beside the village hall.

one year later

1923

A congregation of its own

The Shorewood Presbyterian Society organizes formally. The first service is held on the sixteenth of June.

a year of building

1924

The first building

A wooden church goes up on the corner — the first building this congregation could call its own.

twenty-seven years in the wooden church

Building in brick 1950 – 1999
1951

A new name

Shorewood Presbyterian becomes North Shore Presbyterian Church — a name for the whole north shore, not one village.

the following year

1952

The sanctuary we still use

The colonial brick building is dedicated. Every Sunday since — more than three thousand of them — has been held in this room.

seven years on

1959

Room for the children

The educational wing is added. Godly Play, Sunday School and Compassion Camp all still happen inside it.

forty-three quiet years

Turning outward 2000 – today
2002

The memorial patio

Built in memory of those who came before. From June to Labor Day the whole congregation now worships out here at nine in the morning, beside the garden.

thirteen years

2015

The pineapple

The congregation takes the pineapple as its mark. It is the universal image of hospitality — a promise made in public, on the sign, before anyone walks in.

seven more

2022

One hundred years

The centennial. Four generations of one congregation, on one corner, in a village of fourteen thousand people.

104years so far
3buildings
74years in this sanctuary
1corner, throughout

Help us fill the gaps

The archive has holes in it

A hundred years leaves gaps — the forty-three years between the education wing and the patio are almost entirely undocumented online. If you have photographs, bulletins, or a story from any decade above, the office would be glad of them.
